# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here.

The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versioning follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Apple app: floating monitor on the Mac** ([ADR-0022](docs/adr/0022-mac-floating-monitor.md)). "Floating monitor" in the Window menu (⌥⌘M) and on the Overview opens a small window that stays on top of other windows, can be dragged anywhere and opens where it was left. It starts as the one-line bar, Scan and the state, download, upload and ping, and its arrow opens the table: the three numbers as tiles, the heart-monitor chart and the connection's facts (adapter, Wi‑Fi signal, band and channel, transmit rate (macOS reports no receive rate), router, DNS servers, Internet ping, packet loss and jitter). The look is Glass by default: the screen behind it, blurred by the system, and the window is left out of screenshots and screen sharing while Glass is on; Off, Low and High are solid, or fade while the pointer is elsewhere. Appearance follows the system or is light or dark. Measuring goes on only while it is open. There is no Apps list, because macOS does not let the app see which apps hold connections. Scan shows its result in the main window, where nothing is changed.

## [0.8.0] - 2026-10-08

A floating monitor stays on top of other windows: download, upload and ping on a heart-monitor style chart, the apps holding connections, and the basics of the connection. The Apple app's check button says "Scan", and its bufferbloat check measures faster lines and uses less data on mobile data.

### Added
- **Floating monitor** (SIC-117). "Floating monitor" in the tray menu opens a small frameless window that stays on top of other windows, can be dragged anywhere and reopens where it was left. It starts as a compact one-line bar shaped like a thermometer, one piece of dark tinted glass in either theme: a round bulb on the left (Optimize, a lightning bolt in a ring, its text only in the tooltip) and a thinner tube with the connection's state, download, upload and ping grouped together and an arrow that expands it. The window takes that shape (a window region) and the shapes are drawn just inside it with smooth edges, so nothing around it hides the desktop. It runs the regular check right there: the ring fills with the real progress while a magnifier moves gently inside it (smaller and slower when Windows asks for less motion). When the check finds what the app can fix, the bulb shows the count inside a heavier ring, which opens the result in the main window (the list of changes, the Wi‑Fi and approval notes, and Fix: nothing changes from the bar itself, as ADR-0020 asks); issues the app cannot fix show as an amber ring with their count, and nothing found as a green tick for a few seconds. The arrow turns the bar into the full table, growing away from the screen edge it sits near, and the back arrow in the table brings the bar back to the same spot. In the full table, three tiles (download, upload, ping) pick what the chart shows; the chart is drawn like a heart monitor: the trace sweeps left to right once a minute, overwrites itself behind a short gap and fades as it ages, and a lost ping is marked in red. The Apps tab lists the apps with open connections to other machines, busiest first, with a browser's many processes as one row; it counts connections, not speed, and says so, because Windows gives speed per app only to administrators. The Network tab shows the basics of the connection: status, kind, adapter, Wi‑Fi network, signal, band and channel, link rate, local IP, router with its ping, DNS servers, Internet ping, packet loss and jitter. The window has its own look (Auto, Light or Dark, under the sun button or in Settings) and looks like frosted glass by default: the screen behind it, blurred and tinted, is its background while its text stays solid ([ADR-0021](docs/adr/0021-floating-monitor-glass.md): Windows' own acrylic does not reach WebView2, so the app captures what is behind the window, which Windows then leaves out of screenshots and screen sharing while Glass is on). The other levels are Off, and Low or High, which make the whole window see-through and solid again while the pointer is on it. A "Floating monitor" button on the Overview and a row in Settings open it, and the first start of a new version (an install or an upgrade) opens it once. No public IP lookup and no links to other products. Everything is read-only and needs no Administrator approval. New `GET /api/traffic` (`app/traffic.py`): the interface counters are read once a second only while the window asks, and the reading stops 30 s after it closes. 54 new keys in all 7 catalogs.

### Changed
- **Apple app: the big check button shows a magnifier and "Scan".** iPhone and iPad have no hover, so it only ever showed "Check my connection". The Apple wording `apple.ui.check.start` is new in all 7 catalogs; Windows keeps its text and "Check again" is unchanged. A touch on the button turns its ring 60° clockwise (a finger has no hover, which turns it on a Mac or with a trackpad).

### Fixed
- **Apple apps: the bufferbloat check (#14) fills lines up to 800 Mbps and reports a refused or capped direction** (SIC-95, the Apple side of SIC-93). A direction stops early only at 1 GB and is then marked capped; HTTP 429/403 marks it refused with the server's `Retry-After`, even after part of it ran, and the worker does not ask again. The ramp is dropped by time (the rounds that began in the first 2 s) instead of the first 10 rounds, as on Windows. The Swift diagnosis rule passes all 28 shared vectors.
- **Apple apps: less data on a metered path.** On mobile data or a personal hotspot a direction stops at 250 MB (500 MB in all, so a line above 200 Mbps reads as incomplete) and the confirmation says it uses mobile data. In Low Data Mode the measurement does not run and says why. 3 new keys in all 7 catalogs (`ui.diag.bufferbloat_run_metered`, `ui.diag.bufferbloat_confirm_metered`, `ui.diag.bufferbloat_constrained`).

## [0.7.0] - 2026-10-07

The Overview checks the connection with one round button and shows each check as it runs, turns the low-risk fixes it finds on under one Administrator approval, checks again to show what changed (with an undo), and lists what the app did for you over the last 7 days. Japanese and Korean are no longer offered as languages.

### Added
- **"What Steady did for you" on the Overview** (SIC-103, [ADR-0020](docs/adr/0020-check-fix-result-flow.md) point 8). Under the check card, the last 7 days counted from the log, a line only for what happened: connections the watchdog brought back (only a recovery right after a real action counts, never one in dry-run mode or after the verify window), with the average incident length; changes measured to help (a measured tweak's own check, or the before/after comparison for the others, still on); manual steps whose before/after came out better; switches to the backup connection. Each line opens the Log on its group. With nothing to count it says how long the app has been watching and that there is nothing to report yet. No "time saved" or speed estimates. New `GET /api/value` (`app/value.py`); 1 new key and a reworded empty text in all 7 catalogs.
- **"Fix N of M": the low-risk fixes together, under one UAC prompt** (SIC-102, [ADR-0020](docs/adr/0020-check-fix-result-flow.md) points 5-7). When the check finds problems the app can fix, the big round button becomes "Fix N of M". It opens a sheet listing what it would turn on, all ticked and untickable: the low-risk tweaks for the problems found, then the other low-risk tweaks the run suggests, each with why, a note when Wi‑Fi will drop for a few seconds and one when Windows will ask for approval. Confirming turns them on through the new elevated operation `tweak-enable-many` (`POST /api/tweaks/batch`): its value is only a list of ids, and both the server and the helper refuse anything but known, low-risk, unmeasured tweaks, each once, before writing anything. Each tweak still goes through the manager (backup, verification, events), tweaks that keep the network up go first, and one failing does not stop the others. Then the app checks again and shows what happened: each tweak on, already on, not supported or failed; each problem it was for "Better now" only when the new check shows it, "Measuring" for findings about past days, otherwise "No change yet" with an undo (`tweak-disable-many`); and what is still open. The confirmation sheet can now carry a list and its own badge. 5 new keys in all 7 catalogs; `ui.check.result.pending_body` no longer speaks of drops only.
- **Apple app: "Last 7 days" on the Overview** (SIC-108, [ADR-0020](docs/adr/0020-check-fix-result-flow.md) point 11). Where Windows shows what it fixed, the Apple app shows what it measured over the week, from the minutes stored on the device: how long it measured (only while open), the median latency to the router and the Internet, the loss to the Internet pings, and how often and for how long the Internet was unreachable while the app was open. Under an hour of measurements the card only says how long it has measured. "Open in History" opens the History tab at 7 days. 7 new keys (`ui.week.*`) in the 7 catalogs.
- **"Check my connection" on the Overview** (SIC-101, [ADR-0020](docs/adr/0020-check-fix-result-flow.md)). The Overview's Suggestions list is replaced by a connection check card, centred on one big round button: a solid disc inside a ring of six segments, flat accent colours. With the pointer on it the ring turns and the label gives way to a magnifier (the ring stays still when Windows asks for less motion). While it runs, a thin ring fills as each check really finishes, with the share of checks done in percent and, under it, the check running now and the finished checks tick off below it with their status; the diagnostics job now reports its progress (`progress` on `GET /api/jobs/<id>`: done, total, the check running and every finished one). The result counts only warn/bad results as problems, most serious first, each with its one-line finding, who can fix it ("The app can fix this" / "You can fix this" / "Nothing on this PC fixes this") and the suggestion buttons that already existed. "Nothing to fix" is a green circle with the numbers behind it: latency to the router and the Internet and packet loss over the last 15 minutes, and drops in the last 24 hours. When the latest result is older than 3 hours, "Check again" becomes the big round button and the old result stays below it with its age. `GET /api/suggestions` carries the summary (`check`: problems, count, fixable, ok, total) and `checks_total`. The Fix button that turns the low-risk tweaks on together comes with SIC-102. The two keys the old list used are removed from every catalog.
- **Apple app: "Check my connection" on the Overview** (SIC-107, [ADR-0020](docs/adr/0020-check-fix-result-flow.md) point 11). One big button replaces the check summary. It runs the checks the device has (Mac: Wi‑Fi signal, channel interference, ping quality, DNS, VPN, physical link; iPhone and iPad: ping quality, DNS, VPN) one after another, and refreshes the Diagnostics tab with the results. It is drawn like the Windows card: a 180 pt dial with a solid accent disc inside six ring segments that turn slowly under the pointer on a Mac (still with Reduce Motion), a thin progress ring with the percent of checks done that moves only as each check really finishes, and a status dial of the same shape for the result. A result older than 3 hours turns the big button back into "Check again" and keeps the result below with how long ago it was. Only warnings and errors are counted; each says "You can fix this" or "Nothing on this device fixes this" with the check's advice. "Nothing to fix" shows the median latency to the router and the Internet and the packet loss behind it, and says "N of M checks are fine" when a check could not judge yet. No Fix button and no value card on Apple: the app changes nothing on the device. 3 new keys in the 7 catalogs that have the check flow (`ui.check.recent_note`, `apple.ui.check.idle_body`, `apple.ui.check.kind.none`).
- **Apple app: choose the language in Settings** (SIC-106). A Language picker at the top of Settings offers "Same as system" and every language the app ships, read from the bundle (so a language removed from `app/locales` drops out by itself), each named in its own language and ordered as on Windows. The choice is kept across launches and applies at once to the whole app: one `Localizer` in the SwiftUI environment replaces the one each view made, and the root sets `\.locale` so dates, times and system controls follow it: the chosen language with the system's region and clock (English picked on a device set to Vietnam keeps 24-hour time). A stored language the app no longer has falls back to "Same as system". Problem reports stay in English. 1 new key in all 7 catalogs (`apple.ui.language.auto`, "Same as system" instead of "Same as Windows").

## [0.6.0] - 2026-10-07

The app installs for all users under `Program Files`, so nothing a process without Administrator rights can change runs elevated. Installing and uninstalling each ask once for Administrator approval; an earlier per-user install is replaced and its data kept.

### Security
- **The app installs for all users under `Program Files`** (SIC-97, [ADR-0019](docs/adr/0019-per-machine-install.md)). Up to 0.5.0 it installed into `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs`, which a process without Admin rights can write, and the elevated helper (and a monitor task with highest privileges, at every logon) ran from there: replacing a file in it meant running code as Administrator at the next UAC prompt. Now:
  - Installing asks once for Administrator approval. The elevated part copies the helper's own folder (never a path it is given) to `Program Files`, adds the "Apps & features" entry in HKLM and the all-users Start menu shortcut; the monitor task and the sign-in (tray) shortcut stay per user, created by the user's own installer (so over-the-shoulder UAC does not give them to the administrator's account). When the helper's result cannot come back (over-the-shoulder UAC), the installer checks the effect itself.
  - An earlier per-user install is stopped, its entry, Start menu shortcut and folder removed; measurements, settings and backups carry over.
  - Uninstall: one elevated step restores every tweak and failover metric, then removes the HKLM entry, the all-users shortcut and the program folder (after the uninstaller exits); this user's task and shortcut go too. A failed restore still keeps the app installed.
  - A packaged copy outside `Program Files` never runs elevated: it asks for UAC only to install itself, the helper refuses everything else from there, and the monitor task gets highest privileges only for an exe under `Program Files`. `Program Files` and the all-users Start menu come from `SHGetKnownFolderPath`, not from environment variables.
  - A per-user install's monitor task (which may have highest privileges and point at the old, user-writable folder) is ended and removed by the elevated step, then registered again for the new exe, without highest privileges, before the old folder is deleted.
  - **PowerShell run by the app finds commands in Windows' folders only.** `-NoProfile` stops neither module autoloading from the user's `Documents\WindowsPowerShell\Modules` nor command lookup in the user's `PATH`, which a process without Admin rights can fill (a `Get-CimInstance.ps1` in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Microsoft\WindowsApps` ran instead of the cmdlet, checked on the dev PC): with the elevated helper's rights. `powershell.exe` is now started by its full path with `PATH` and the module path limited to Windows' folders, and every script sets both again first, with .NET calls only.
  - `install --target` is gone. 8 new keys and 2 changed in all 9 catalogs; 4 keys that nothing used any more removed.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-10-07

`dns_fastest` now compares the public DNS with the DNS already in use and switches only when that helps; a refusal comes before the UAC prompt.

### Changed
- **`dns_fastest` switches only when it helps** (SIC-96, ADR-0015 point 8). It benchmarks the DNS in use as well as the public servers, on common names and on names no resolver has cached, and switches only when the DNS in use is broken or the chosen public server is at least 20% and 10 ms faster on both. Otherwise it is refused with the numbers and nothing is written. The check runs before the UAC prompt (new hook `Tweak.preflight`, used by the server before elevating) and again inside `apply()`. On the dev PC, where the router's DNS answers common names in ~7 ms, it now declines (it had made DNS ~3× slower, EXP-021). 1 new key in all 9 catalogs. The tweak stays experimental until it has been measured again.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-10-07

An upload limit that keeps latency low under load (measured: p95 under upload about halved), tweaks whose value comes from a measurement, an MTU tweak, a DNS tweak and three network-stack tweaks (all experimental), two diagnostics checks (the route to far-away destinations and the path MTU), a bufferbloat check that fills lines up to 800 Mbps, and backups the elevated helper can trust: validated before restore and kept where only Administrators can write.

### Added
- **Tweak `dns_fastest`** (SIC-87, experimental, [ADR-0015](docs/adr/0015-tweak-value-from-measurement.md)): switches the uplink's DNS to the fastest public servers over HTTPS.
  - At apply time it benchmarks Cloudflare, Google and Quad9 (the providers Windows has a DoH template for) and keeps two different providers, the winner's two servers and the runner-up's fastest; DoH auto-upgrade is turned on for them (with fallback to plain UDP).
  - The backup holds whether DNS was static or from DHCP and the DoH flags of all six candidates; turning it off resets to DHCP (`-ResetServerAddresses`) or puts the static servers back, and removes DoH entries that did not exist.
  - Not offered (and never suggested) with a VPN, on a domain, with a DNS suffix, with a static IPv6 DNS or behind a captive portal. A hand-made setup counts as on.
  - The DNS-change warning of the monitor no longer fires for a change the app made in the last 5 minutes; it is recorded as `dns_observed` (`event.dns_app_changed`).
  - A broken DNS in diagnostics check #6 now points at this tweak.
- **Experimental tweaks `tcp_ecn`, `rsc_off`, `packet_coalescing_off`** (SIC-88): TCP ECN on, Receive Segment Coalescing off on the Wi‑Fi card, Packet Coalescing Filter off. All off by default, labelled experimental, with the original value in the backup. `tcp_ecn` goes back to Windows' own `disabled` even without a backup; the other two need the backup. A card without RSC is "not supported". `app/winsys.py` gained the system calls behind them.
- **Diagnostics check #15, route to far-away destinations** (SIC-89): TCP connect times to five widely served sites; one that takes at least 3× and 80 ms more than the nearest is reported as a detour (`info`), and the new manual step `tunnel_route` suggests considering a tunnel service (no product named) with the usual before/after measurement. Not measured while a VPN or tunnel is up.
- Manual step `router_sqm` now explains why (the queue builds on the download side), names OpenWrt's `sqm-scripts` for a router without SQM and a faster plan for a line that is simply full. All new texts are in the 9 catalogs.
- **Tweak `mtu_pmtu`: match the MTU to the largest packet the path carries** (SIC-86, ADR-0016, `docs/TWEAKS.md` section 4). Turning it on runs the path MTU measurement of check #16 (about 1 s, a few dozen small packets). It then lowers the IPv4 MTU of the uplink interface to the measured path MTU. This happens only when large packets vanish silently at two or more destinations, with no VPN, no jumbo frames and a path of at least 1280 bytes; otherwise nothing is written and no UAC prompt is shown. Written with `netsh interface ipv4 set subinterface <index> mtu=<value> store=persistent`. The backup names the interface by GUID, so turning it off restores that interface's MTU even after the traffic moved elsewhere. It then measures again and records whether the path now carries the new MTU. Check #16 links the tweak when it warns.
- **Diagnostic #16, Path MTU** (SIC-86): finds the largest packet that crosses the path with "do not fragment" pings (`app/pmtu.py`, `IcmpSendEcho`, no Admin, no `ping.exe`) to 1.1.1.1, 8.8.8.8 and 9.9.9.9, up to the interface MTU or 1500. It warns when the interface MTU is larger and large packets vanish silently: the PPPoE case (1492 behind a 1500 Wi‑Fi interface) where pages hang half-loaded and downloads stall. When routers answer "packet too big", connections adapt, so that is only info. It runs in the default diagnostics run: about 1 s, or a few seconds when large packets vanish silently. It is read-only; the tweak `mtu_pmtu` (above) acts on it.
- **Measured tweaks** (SIC-84, [ADR-0016](docs/adr/0016-measured-tweaks.md)): a tweak whose value is computed from a measurement of the network in use, taken just before it is turned on. The measurement is stored with the backup (values, time, a hashed id of the router; no SSID, MAC or IP address). The elevated helper receives the measurement, not the value, and works the value out itself. The tweak is refused before any UAC prompt when there is nothing to fix. A changed network or a measurement older than 30 days is flagged in the UI, never re-measured or rewritten automatically.
- **Upload limit against bufferbloat** (`upload_shaping`, SIC-85): measures the upload (~15 s, without the download phase of check #14), then throttles all outbound traffic to 85% of it with a `NetQosPolicy` of the tool's own name (`StableInternet-Upload`, 1–1000 Mbps). Refused when latency rises less than 30 ms under upload. After applying it measures again and records whether it helped (`tweak_verified` event); if it did not, the result suggests turning it off. Turning it off removes exactly that policy. The bufferbloat check now suggests it when the upload direction is bloated. 27 new keys in all 9 catalogs. Uses the default (`localhost`) policy store: `-PolicyStore PersistentStore` is not a NetQos store. On the dev PC (EXP-015) the measurement refused, since latency rose only 25 ms under upload, and the QoS create, re-rate and remove commands worked in both stores.

### Changed
- **`dns_fastest` ships as experimental** (SIC-91, EXP-021). On the dev PC it made DNS about 3× slower than the router's own DNS (uncached lookups ~270 ms vs ~80 ms, popular names ~75 ms vs 12–29 ms): its benchmark ranks the public providers against each other but never against the DNS already in use. Measured the same session: `upload_shaping` about halves p95 latency under upload (EXP-019), and `mtu_pmtu` correctly declines when path MTU discovery works (EXP-020).
- Check #16: the advice leaves out the IPv6 `netsh` command when the path is below 1280 bytes (IPv6's minimum), and the no-IPv4 summary says it can mean the PC is offline.
- `enable_measured` takes the verification right after applying from the tweak (`MeasuredTweak.verdict`), so each measured tweak judges its own effect; the upload limit's verdict and messages are unchanged.

### Fixed
- **The bufferbloat check (#14) can fill a line faster than 80 Mbps, and no longer calls a half measurement "ok"** (SIC-93).
  - A load direction stopped at 100 MB in its 10 s, so it could never read more than 80 Mbps; on the dev PC every run read 79–80 Mbps on a line that uploads about 230. A faster line's queue was never filled and the check said "ok" without having tested it. A direction now runs for its 10 s and stops early only at 1 GB (800 Mbps); a direction that reaches that limit is marked, and its result counts as a lower bound. The upload limit's ceiling refusal moves with it.
  - speed.cloudflare.com limits test traffic per address (HTTP 429, or 403, after a few runs, with a `Retry-After` of about 30 minutes). A refused direction is now reported as refused, with the wait it asked for, even when part of it had run, instead of a load error; the check does not work around the limit.
  - "Ok" now needs both directions measured and fully loaded; otherwise the result is info "incomplete". A rise that was measured is still reported as warn/bad. The shared diagnosis vectors and the Swift port agree.
  - The data used is shown as "about 250 MB per 100 Mbps of line speed, at most 2 GB" (was "up to 200 MB"). 6 new keys and 2 changed ones in all 9 catalogs.
- **The Wi‑Fi tweaks find the driver property by its `RegistryKeyword`, so a translated driver or Windows no longer reads as "the card does not have this property"** (SIC-94). A property is looked up by keyword first (the order each tweak lists, `*` and case ignored) and by its English `DisplayName` only as a fallback. The value to write is the registry value recorded for that keyword when the driver lists it, so it does not depend on translated `DisplayValue` text; it is recorded only where it was read from a card (the dev PC's MediaTek MT7922) or fixed by Microsoft (`*WakeOnMagicPacket`, `*WakeOnPattern`). For Intel and Realtek only the keyword is listed, so a translated value there is still "no suitable value". If the English text and the recorded registry value disagree (the same keyword numbered differently), nothing is written. Unreadable or translated `netsh` output is "cannot tell which band", never "not connected" (`docs/TWEAKS.md`, "How a property and its value are matched").
- **`upload_shaping` no longer takes the measurement's own ceiling for the line's speed.** A bufferbloat load phase stops at 100 MB, so it cannot report more than 80 Mbps; on a faster line the cap would have been about 68 Mbps. A measurement at 95% of that ceiling or more is now refused, before any write or UAC prompt. Raising the ceiling itself is SIC-93.
- **`upload_shaping` works on the badly bloated lines it is for** (review of SIC-85). The ramp of a load phase is dropped by time instead of by 10 rounds (on a bloated line a round takes most of a second, so 10 rounds ate most of the phase); a ping lost under load counts as the 900 ms timeout instead of being left out; and "too few samples" counts pings attempted, not answered. Simulated 400–1900 ms lines were refused or not measured before; they now get a cap.
- **`upload_shaping` no longer limits the local network.** The throttle matches all outbound traffic, so a NAS copy or casting ran at the Internet upload's speed. Six unthrottled, more specific policies (`StableInternet-Upload-Local1…6`: private, link-local and unique-local prefixes) now exempt the LAN; they are created before the throttle and removed after it. Still to be checked on a real machine.
- With the upload limit on, check #14 says it measures through the limit and asks to re-measure when the upload reaches it; a limit with no measurement on record is flagged; a measured enable and check #14 never load the line together; the restore only accepts a backup naming the tool's own policies; an unreadable state no longer measures or shows a UAC prompt; the confirmation says the measurement runs twice and how much data it moves for the line's speed. 3 new keys in all 9 catalogs.
- **The two Wi‑Fi tweaks of 0.3.0 no longer claim a setting the driver ships with, and `wifi_prefer_5g` no longer touches a 5 or 6 GHz connection** (SIC-83 review).
  - A driver property that already holds the target because of the driver's default (the MediaTek card's `PreferredBand` = Prefer 5GHz and `TxPowerLevel` = Highest) was listed as on although the tool never set it, and turning it off ran `Reset-NetAdapterAdvancedProperty`: the Wi‑Fi dropped, the same value came back and the switch stayed on. Without a backup it is now shown as on by the driver default, its switch is disabled, and turning it off writes nothing. After any reset to the default the value is read back and a value that did not change is reported as a failure.
  - `wifi_prefer_5g` is offered only while the card is on a 2.4 GHz access point (the `Band` field of `netsh wlan show interfaces`, or the connected BSSID's band in the scan; a channel number cannot tell 2.4 GHz from 6 GHz). On 5 GHz it would only cost a reconnect, and on 6 GHz (Wi‑Fi 6E/7) it could pull the card down. The connection is read from the interface with the tweak's adapter name, and a hidden network is matched by the connected BSSID.

- **`dns_fastest` restore after the uplink changed** (review of the 0.4.0 work): the backup now holds the interface's GUID, and turning the tweak off, and checking the result, look at that interface instead of whichever one is the uplink at that moment. Turning it on while the backup belongs to another interface is refused until it has been turned off, so a second original is never lost. Turning it off also works offline, with an unreadable uplink or a DoH list that cannot be read at that moment, and says so when the adapter is gone. New hooks `Tweak.recapture` and `Tweak.backup_conflict`.
- `dns_fastest` while on now checks the network again: a VPN, a domain, a DNS suffix or a captive portal that appeared since shows a "Worth a look" warning beside the switch (the captive-portal probe is reused for a minute).
- `dns_fastest` restore only undoes what the tweak could have changed: a DoH entry somebody else has changed is left alone, and nothing already as it was is rewritten. DoH templates with `{?dns}` or a query string are accepted and only ever reach PowerShell as base64. A Windows without the DoH cmdlets reads as "not supported" instead of an error.
- `tcp_ecn` is read through `Get-NetTCPSetting`, so it works on a non-English Windows (the `netsh show` labels are translated), and turning it off without a backup sets Windows' own `default` instead of `disabled`.

New hook `Tweak.backup_reading`.

### Security
- **The elevated helper no longer restores values from a tampered `backup.json`** ([ADR-0017](docs/adr/0017-validate-backup-before-restore.md)). The file is writable without Admin, so a process of the user could make the helper write any HKLM DWORD (registry tweaks), any DoH template URL and DNS servers (`dns_fastest`), or another adapter, property, power setting or `netsh` setting, the next time the user approved turning a tweak off or uninstalled.
  - Every tweak kind has `check_original(system, original)`: fixed setting names must match the declaration, the registry path, Wi‑Fi card and property keyword must be the ones the tweak resolves now, and values must be ones the setting accepts (a DWORD, a power index in the setting's range, the property's `ValidRegistryValues`, `TCP_GLOBAL_VALUES` / `OFFLOAD_VALUES`, unicast IPv4 DNS servers). The base class refuses everything, so a kind without a check fails closed.
  - `upload_shaping` restores only its own `StableInternet-Upload` policy, at a rate within 1–1000 Mbps, and only its own `StableInternet-Upload-Local1`…`Local6` LAN exemptions.
  - `mtu_pmtu` restores only an MTU of 1280–1500 on an interface named by a well-formed GUID.
  - A Wi‑Fi property backup must name exactly the `RegistryKeyword` the tweak resolves on the card (found by keyword or name, so translated drivers still pass).
  - `dns_fastest` accepts DoH entries only for its six candidate addresses with each provider's own template, and always restores the built-in template.
  - Turning a tweak off with a refused entry restores nothing, keeps the backup and logs `tweak.result.backup_rejected` (level `bad`). `adopt_backup` uses the same check. Failover's metric restore refuses an entry that is not `{"automatic": true}` or a metric in 1–9999 (`failover.result.backup_rejected`). 2 new keys in all 9 catalogs.
  - Residual risk: a forged `dns_fastest` entry can still choose which unicast IPv4 DNS servers come back and on which interface, and a forged `mtu_pmtu` entry which interface gets an MTU of 1280–1500. Closing it needs backups the user cannot write (ADR-0018).
- **Backups moved to `HKLM\SOFTWARE\Ambysto\Steady`, where only Administrators can write** ([ADR-0018](docs/adr/0018-backups-in-hklm.md), amends ADR-0017). This closes the residual risk above: a process without Admin can no longer choose the DNS servers, the interface or the MTU (or anything else) that the elevated helper restores.
  - The whole backup is one JSON value (`Backup`, REG_SZ, 64-bit view). Unelevated processes still read it (whether a tweak has a backup, the preferred failover path, whether uninstalling needs UAC); writing it without Admin fails.
  - The first elevated process on the machine that reads backups imports `data/backup.json` once, whether it exists, is corrupt or not, and sets `LegacyImported`: no `backup.json` is read by an elevated process again, at any path, also after a reinstall. Every operation of the elevated helper reads the backups first, so the first UAC prompt closes the import. Imported are entries that pass the tweak's `check_original` and whose change is still in effect (new `Tweak.backup_in_effect`: `read()` or `backup_reading()`; for `dns_fastest` the named interface must have the tweak's DNS, for `mtu_pmtu` its MTU must be below the saved one), and failover metrics whose switch is still in effect (a manual metric other than the saved one), never over an entry the store has. Refused entries, or ones that could not be judged (Wi‑Fi card off), wait in the `Quarantine` value and are tried again once by each elevated process. Nothing is renamed in the user's folder. Until the import an unelevated reader shows both.
  - The uninstaller's elevated `restore-all` deletes `Backup` and `Quarantine` once everything is restored (the key and `LegacyImported` stay); uninstalling asks for UAC when either value exists. New key `installer.store_kept` in all 9 catalogs.
  - An elevated process of the packaged build ignores `STABLEINTERNET_DATA` and `STABLEINTERNET_USERDIR` (user environment variables reach it).
  - `backup_lock` is re-entrant within a thread. Tests keep the file backend with `STABLEINTERNET_BACKUP=file` (set in `tests/__init__.py`); the packaged build ignores it.

An `internet_down` incident now opens only when both ping and the latest probe round fail; failing pings while the probe succeeds are no longer an incident. Diagnostic #5 distinguishes "ICMP rate-limited" from "real outage" and from ISP-side faults. Configured in `settings.json` → `probes`.
- API/UI server (`app/server.py`, ADR-0005): runs inside the monitor, with normal rights, `127.0.0.1:47613`. Checks `Host` (against DNS rebinding), a random per-startup token embedded in the page (`X-Token`, constant-time comparison), rejects cross-origin writes, returns no CORS, CSP/anti-framing/CORP, 64 KB body limit and 10s timeout; slow work runs as background jobs. Operations that need Admin run in the child process `app/elevated.py` via UAC (`app/elevation.py`), which accepts only a fixed list of commands. Verified in a real browser: a foreign page cannot read the token, cannot call the API and cannot embed the UI.
- Watchdog (`app/watchdog.py`, `app/actions.py`, ADR-0004, `docs/WATCHDOG.md`): runs in the monitor process, **off by default**. Reacts to `wifi_down` and `router_unreachable` (including silent hangs) after 15s; step 1 reconnects (no Admin needed; disconnects and then reconnects on a silent hang), step 2 restarts the adapter (Admin). Does not intervene when the fault is at the ISP, when a wired network is in use, when the user disconnected Wi‑Fi themselves, within 60s after the machine wakes, or within 120s after a tweak change. Limits: 120s spacing, at most 6 times/hour (counted across restarts too, from the log), turns itself off after 3 consecutive ineffective attempts. `dry_run` mode only records "what it would do". Enabling/disabling in `settings.json` takes effect without a restart.
- Tweak framework (`app/tweaks.py`, `app/winsys.py`, ADR-0003): 4 primitive types (adapter advanced property, HKLM registry DWORD, powercfg AC/DC, adapter binding). Enable: writes `backup.json` **before** applying, reads back to verify, rolls back automatically on error or when it has no effect. Disable: restores and compares against the backup; without a backup it only uses a known default (powercfg refuses). `adopt_backup()` accepts an external backup (e.g. EXP-001) for an already enabled tweak. `python -m app.tweaks list|enable|disable` only prints the plan by default; `--apply` is required to write. The concrete tweak catalog is not declared yet (SIC-18/19/20). Every string passed to PowerShell goes through base64.
- `app/diagnostics.py`: 13 read-only checks per `docs/DIAGNOSTICS.md` (driver, signal, channel interference, drop history, ping quality, DNS, TCP port exhaustion, VPN, wired network, tweaks not enabled, Wi‑Fi 7/MLO, Wi‑Fi modem bridge, physical link). Each check separates a pure evaluation function from the data collection; a check with no data returns `info`, never `ok`. Run with `python -m app.diagnostics [--only 2,13] [--json] [--compare] [--no-save]`; results are saved in the `diagnostic_runs` table. Added to `winutil`: network scan, driver store, DNS servers, Windows events (a single PowerShell command). The thresholds of #11 and #13 are calibrated on the 2026-10-03 data, see `docs/DIAGNOSTICS.md`. Tests in `tests/test_diagnostics.py`.
- `app/autostart.py` + `scripts/monitor/run_monitor.pyw`: starts the monitor automatically at sign-in via Task Scheduler, runs hidden through `pythonw.exe`, logs to `data/monitor.log`. `python -m app.autostart status|install|uninstall`; `install`/`uninstall` only print the commands that would run, and `--apply` must be added to change the machine. Default privilege `LIMITED`, `--highest` when Admin is needed.
- `app/singleton.py`: prevents two monitor instances from running at the same time using a named mutex (no "stale PID" problem as with a PID file). The mutex name is tied to the data folder: only two monitors writing the same `metrics.db` block each other. `python -m app.monitor` exits immediately if another instance is already running; adds `--log-file` (rotating) for running without a console.

- **Check #3 on 6 GHz** (SIC-71), Windows and Apple: a crowded 6 GHz channel got the 2.4 GHz advice ("move to 5 GHz or channels 1/6/11"). It now gets `diag.interference.advice_6g`: another 6 GHz channel, or a narrower width. 1 new shared vector, and the new key in all 9 catalogs.
- **Check #5 names jitter as jitter** (SIC-66), on Windows and Apple. When a target warns only for jitter above 30 ms, the summary used to say packet loss. It now says latency is unstable:
  - the router: `diag.ping.router_jitter`, with an Apple wording;
  - the Internet with ping only: `diag.ping.wan_jitter`;
  - the Internet with clean TCP/HTTP: `diag.ping.icmp_jitter`.

- **The default route is found while a WireGuard-style VPN is up** (SIC-72): such a client leaves 0.0.0.0/0 alone and adds 0.0.0.0/1 + 128.0.0.0/1, so `GetBestRoute(0.0.0.0)` matched the /1 route and `default_route_native()` returned nothing. Check #4's gateway lookup then ran PowerShell every time and the DNS-change watch (needs the route) paused while the VPN was on. When the best route is not a default one, the route table is read and its lowest-metric 0.0.0.0/0 route with a gateway is used: the physical one. 5 new tests on fake route tables.
- **Windows records a VPN starting or stopping** (SIC-68): the monitor asks Windows (`GetBestRoute`, a native call) which interface carries traffic to a public address every 2 s and, when that moves to another interface (a full-tunnel VPN, wired <-> Wi‑Fi), writes a `route_change` event at that moment, even when the gateway stays the same or the VPN's route is on-link (WireGuard clients add 0.0.0.0/1 + 128.0.0.0/1, which hides the default route), and re-reads the gateway at once so `gateway_change` is no longer up to 30 s late. Check #5 leaves out the minutes around `route_change` like the other network-change events, and the Log shows it with the outages. A route that vanishes is not a switch; a route that returns on another interface is.
- **New app icon on Windows**: the window's title bar and taskbar button, the exe, the Start menu shortcut and Apps & features now show the Apple app's icon (white Wi‑Fi arcs and "A"/"S" on a dark gray tile) instead of the green tray square or the Python icon. - Internet drops that start within 60 s of the previous one's recovery are reported as one unstable episode (SIC-69, ADR-0014): one `internet_down` event spans them and says "unstable: N drops", instead of several short events. The event is written up to 60 s after the last recovery; the tray state, toasts and the watchdog still follow the live state. 2 new `event.internet_down.*.unstable` keys in all 9 catalogs.
- **Check #5 "Ping quality" leaves out the minutes of a network change** (issue #2, SIC-61), on Windows and Apple: the minute of the change and the next one, where pings fail or take two routes and the check blamed the ISP (seen after a VPN toggle: ~257 ms jitter, 12.5% of TCP probes lost, `warn` "ISP/WAN problem"). Windows uses the monitor's `gateway_change`, `roam` and `wifi_state` events; the Apple app records switches from one working route to another (VPN on/off, another network, another router) in the same `events` table; losing the connection and getting the same route back still counts, since the Apple app has no check #4 to report it. - **`scripts/link_calibration.py` no longer stops with `UnicodeEncodeError` when its output is piped or redirected on Windows** (SIC-80), as in `python scripts/link_calibration.py db | more` or `> table.txt`. A Windows pipe or redirect uses the ANSI code page (cp1252), which cannot print the `≤`, `≥` and `–` of the table. The table is now plain ASCII (`<=6 & loss>=5%`, `-` for a period without data), so it prints the same everywhere. A new test prints it through a cp1252 pipe. Printing straight to a console was never affected.
- **The 4 `tests/test_link_calibration.py` tests failed on Windows** (SIC-78) with `PermissionError: [WinError 32]` while deleting the temporary SQLite file, and would have stopped the release workflow, which runs the tests on `windows-latest`. `with sqlite3.connect(...)` only ends a transaction; it does not close the connection, and Windows cannot delete an open file. `scripts/link_calibration.py` (`minutes()`) and the test's `setUp` now use `contextlib.closing`. The whole suite is clean on Windows (724 tests).
- **`test_naming` failed on CI** (SIC-79) with `UnicodeDecodeError: ... 0xa7` and would have stopped the release workflow. It scanned `scripts/` with `*.py*`, which also matches the `.pyc` files that tests importing a script leave in `scripts/__pycache__` (a Python 3.11 `.pyc` starts with byte `0xa7`). It now scans `*.py` and `*.pyw`. The error never showed on the development machine because `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1` is set there, so no `.pyc` is written; run the suite without that variable to see what CI sees.
- **Apple app: accessibility and other languages** (SIC-75), checked on the iPhone Simulator in German at the largest accessibility text size and in Japanese:

- **Apple app on a real iPhone** (SIC-67), two bugs the Simulator could not show:
  - On mobile data the carrier's gateway was taken for the router, pinged (it never answers: "100% lost") and shown with its addresses. Check #5 would have blamed the local network. Mobile data now has no router to measure, show or use for the DNS check.
  - A ping or TCP round that ran while iOS had paused the app (in the background, or locked) counted the pause as round-trip time: jitter of ~2.7 s at 0% loss. A round that takes longer than its timeout plus 0.5 s is now dropped, neither a reply nor a loss. - Failover could not change the real metric: `interface_metric_set` was mistakenly placed in the `System` Protocol instead of `WindowsSystem`, so every real switch/restore (monitor, UI button, Admin process, uninstall) failed with `AttributeError` even though tests with the fake system still passed. Added a test requiring `WindowsSystem` to have every operation of `System`.
- Failover: a failed switch/restore (e.g. the backup adapter was unplugged) no longer retries and logs an error every 5 seconds (measured 58 error events in 5 minutes, using up the entire toast quota). It now reports once and retries after 60 s → 5 minutes → 15 minutes; the UI still has the "Switch back" button for an unplugged path.
- Failover: clicking "Switch now" while failover was off was automatically rolled back after 5 seconds; it is now the user's choice (`source: manual`) and is kept. The old IP after DHCP changes the IP is no longer counted as a "failed path"; time the machine spends asleep is no longer counted as down/healthy time.
- `backup.json`: two processes (monitor, Admin process) writing at the same time could lose each other's original values. Each write now holds an inter-process lock and changes only its own entry (`config.backup_lock`, `config.put_backup_entry`).

Reinstalling keeps the task's "highest" privilege if the user had enabled it. `--yes` no longer shows the result dialog (writes to `installer.log`, now UTF-8).
- Watchdog: when Wi‑Fi was lost but the PC was still online via a USB-tethered phone (media "Unspecified") or USB 4G, the dry run still recorded "would reconnect Wi‑Fi". Previously only 802.3 wired adapters counted as another path, and an adapter plugged in after the list was read (once every 5 minutes) was treated as Wi‑Fi. Now every physical non-Wi‑Fi adapter that is Up counts as another path; for an unknown adapter the list is re-read immediately, once per adapter.
- Failover (found while testing for real with an iPhone): the "Switch now" button was rendered from stale state, so the user "switched" to the path already in use and its metric was lowered further (Wi‑Fi = 1). Now the server and the Admin process re-read the routes at click time and reject this before asking for UAC (`failover.result.in_use`). - Server: rejecting a write request without first draining the body (body too large, missing token, wrong origin or wrong host) made Windows abort the connection ~1 in 10 times, so the client saw "connection aborted" instead of an error code. It now drains the body (up to 1 MB and 3 seconds) before returning the error.
- Monitor: when the machine slept/froze during an incident, the first successful ping after waking closed the incident with a duration equal to the whole sleep (reproduced: a bogus 28,804 s incident after 8 hours of sleep). Now a gap > 30 s between two ticks (or 10× the ping interval) cuts the open incident off at the last tick, resets the counters and records a `monitor_gap` event.

- `ping-logger.ps1`: misclassified `internet_down` when the router also lost connectivity during the same incident.
