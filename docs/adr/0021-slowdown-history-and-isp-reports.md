# ADR-0021: Slowdown history and periodic ISP reports

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-08
- **Related:** [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md), [DIAGNOSTICS.md](../DIAGNOSTICS.md), [ADR-0014](0014-merge-internet-drops.md), [ADR-0001](0001-python-stdlib-web-ui.md), [ADR-0012](0012-user-sent-problem-reports.md), [PRIVACY.md](../../PRIVACY.md)

## Context

On 2026-10-07 the download fell from about 300 Mb/s to about 5 Mb/s while everything else looked healthy: ping 6 ms, no packet loss, TCP and HTTP probes passing, upload 122 Mb/s, Wi‑Fi link rate and signal normal. The monitor recorded nothing, because an incident is only declared when the router or the Internet stops answering (ping plus probes). A throttled or congested line is therefore invisible to the app, and it is the case where a user most needs proof for the provider: "it was not my PC, not my Wi‑Fi, and it happened on these dates for this long".

The user wants a history of such episodes, kept long enough to be summarised into weekly, monthly and quarterly reports to attach to a complaint. Per the business model (Pro / supporter tier: ISP report, long history; the free build stays fully functional, no ads), the reports are a paid feature.

## Decision

1. **Throughput samples** (`speedsample.py`, `speedwatch.py`). On a schedule the monitor downloads about 10 MB and uploads about 2 MB from the large CDN the bufferbloat check already uses, one connection each, timed from the first byte of the body so the TLS handshake and first round trip do not count. Off by default; the user turns it on in Settings, which says how much data it uses: about 12 MB every `speed.interval_min` minutes (default 30), so about **17 GB a month** at the default; 15, 60 and 120 minutes are offered too. A refusal by the server (HTTP 429/403) backs the schedule off to what `Retry-After` asked, or twice the interval. A sample is not taken (the row is kept with a reason code) during an outage, within 2 minutes of a route change, or when the PC itself was moving other traffic during the sample (the interface counters show more than the sample's own bytes): the PC would be competing with itself. Samples are kept for 2 years (a row is ~100 bytes).
2. **Baseline** (`slowdown.baseline`). Per network (gateway + Wi‑Fi name): the plan speed if the user entered one, otherwise the median of the best quarter of the last 14 days' samples of that network, so a long slow stretch cannot pull it down to itself. No baseline until 6 samples exist.
3. **Slowdown episode** (`slowdown.SlowdownTracker`, one per direction). It opens at the second consecutive sample below 50% of the baseline (one low sample is noise), starts at the first of the two, stays open while samples are below 70%, and ends at the first sample at or above 70%. A pause between samples longer than three intervals, a change of network or the monitor stopping ends it at its last sample (what happened meanwhile is not known). A sample that was skipped never opens, extends or ends an episode.
4. **Evidence and verdict** (`slowdown.verdict`). When a sample is taken the monitor snapshots what it knows: loss and latency to the router over the last minute (needs ≥ 20 pings, else unknown), the Wi‑Fi link rate in that direction and RSSI, the speed of the other direction, and whether the Internet route leaves by another interface than the default route (a VPN or tunnel). One verdict per episode, from the snapshot at its start and at its end: `local` when router loss ≥ 5%, RSSI < −76 dBm or the link rate ≤ 30 Mb/s (the thresholds of diagnostics #11/#13); `outside` when the router hop was clean; `unknown` when a tunnel was on, there was no router data, or the two snapshots disagree. `outside` means outside the PC ↔ router hop: the router itself, the line or the provider. The app cannot tell those apart, and a report says so.
5. **Storage** (schema v4). `speed_samples` and `slowdowns` (open episodes have `end_ts` NULL; a monitor that starts closes those a previous run left open at that run's last sample). Slowdowns, outage events (`internet_down`, `router_down`), `slowdown` events and the monitor's own `monitor_start` / `monitor_stop` / `monitor_gap` events are kept for 2 years for every user, free or paid, so someone who subscribes later still has the evidence; everything else keeps the 30 days of `retention_days`. The start/stop/gap events are what lets a report say when nothing was watching.
6. **Reports** (`report.py`, `POST /api/report`, Pro). For a calendar week (Monday first), month or quarter, in local time; a period still running is reported up to now. The server writes, in the results folder under `reports/`, a self-contained print-friendly HTML page (no script, no external file; the user presses Ctrl+P and saves as PDF, so no PDF library is added, ADR-0001), a CSV of the outages and slowdowns, and a CSV of every sample including the skipped ones with their reason, and opens the page in the default browser. The page holds: the period and time zone (local, with UTC in brackets), the plan speed, optional provider and public IP typed by the user, a summary (availability over the time the app was watching, outages, slowdowns by verdict, time not watched), median / slowest 10% / normal speed per direction and the share of samples below half, the slowdown and outage tables with the evidence of each slowdown, a download-by-hour-of-day chart, the method in plain words and a SHA-256 over the listed rows (independent of the language) so a later edit is detectable. No BSSID, MAC address, Wi‑Fi name or local IP is included. Nothing is uploaded; the user sends the files themselves ([ADR-0012](0012-user-sent-problem-reports.md)).
7. **What is free.** Sampling, detection, the speed section of the Log (state, slowdowns going on, the latest sample) and the events in the Log (30 days) stay in the free build. Pro adds the history beyond 30 days and the reports. The gate is `license.is_supporter()`, in GPL code. It is a stub that returns `True` until the key check exists (Polar.sh, its own ADR), so for now everything is open; the change that adds the real check must not be mixed with a feature change.
8. **Not in this decision.** Notifying the provider automatically, any server-side component, a PDF library, and a mobile version (the schema is kept compatible so [ADR-0011](0011-apple-measurement-history.md) can add it later).

## Consequences

- ✅ A throttled line becomes a recorded, dated fact with the evidence next to it, which is what the user can take to the provider.
- ✅ Free users keep detection; paying users get the long view and the document. Nothing the app does for the connection is locked.
- ✅ Everything is computed on the user's machine and stays there.
- ⚠️ Sampling uses about 17 GB a month at the default and may be rate-limited by the test server; it is off by default and backs off. A gap in samples is shown as a gap and counted as unmonitored, never filled in.
- ⚠️ The verdict cannot separate the router from the provider. A guided wired-cable test (a manual step whose result is typed into the report) is the planned way to rule out Wi‑Fi and the router's wireless side; it is not part of this change.
- ⚠️ A baseline needs 6 samples (3 hours at the default) before the first episode can be declared, unless the plan speed is entered. A line that has been slow since the first sample, with no plan speed, learns the slow speed as normal.
- ⚠️ Throughput is measured against one CDN with one connection per direction; a result below a plan's headline speed does not prove a breach of contract by itself, and the report is worded as evidence of what was measured, not as a verdict on the contract.
