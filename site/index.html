The problem

A fast speed test doesn’t mean a stable connection.

A speed test measures a short burst of throughput. Browsing, calls and games depend on something else: whether every packet arrives, on time.

Wi‑Fi

Signal is not flow

Bars measure signal strength. A driver hang, interference or a crowded channel can stall your traffic while all four bars stay lit — and Windows often records nothing.

Your provider

Fast at home, slow abroad

Domestic speed can be excellent while some international routes are congested or shaped, or while your provider’s DNS servers are slow. It is not always your equipment.

Time of day

Evening peaks

Shared networks — apartment Wi‑Fi, mobile towers — get busy when everyone is online, and latency and packet loss climb with them.

Ambysto Steady can’t make your provider faster. It tells you which part of the path is failing, and fixes the parts that are yours to fix.

Sample data

Continuous monitoring

Pings your router and the Internet every second, plus TCP and HTTP probes every 10 s.

14 diagnostic checks

Driver, signal, channel interference, drop history, DNS, bufferbloat, Wi‑Fi 7/MLO compatibility, a modem still broadcasting in bridge mode, blocked antennas and more — with before/after results.

Watchdog for silent stalls

Reconnects, then restarts the adapter if needed. Off by default, rate-limited, and it switches itself off if it isn’t helping.

Reversible tweaks

On/off switches for adapter power saving, PCIe power management, TCP port reuse, IPv6 and more. The original value is saved first and restored on failure; each effect is measured.

Backup connection

When the main path loses the Internet and another works — Ethernet, a USB 4G dongle, your phone’s hotspot — it can switch over and back. Off by default.

Notifications & log

A notification when an outage lasts 30 seconds or more and when it ends, and a full log of incidents, your changes and every watchdog action.

Safety & privacy

Built to never make things worse.

Every change goes through the same steps: back up the original value, apply, verify, and roll back automatically if it didn’t work. Uninstalling restores everything.

No account, no telemetry, no analytics, no advertising. The privacy policy lists every connection the app makes.

  • Backed up first

    Original values are saved before any change and restored when you switch a tweak off.

  • Capped

    The watchdog acts at most six times an hour and stops if three attempts in a row don’t help.

  • Local only

    The app talks to its own server on 127.0.0.1 with a per-session token. Nothing is reachable from other computers.

  • Asks first

    Anything that needs Administrator rights runs separately and asks through the Windows UAC prompt every time.

FAQ

Questions people ask.

Will it make my Internet faster?

No. It can’t increase what your provider delivers. It finds what stops you from getting that speed consistently — interference, a misbehaving driver, a settings conflict, slow DNS — and tells you when the problem is on your provider’s side.

Why does it ask for Administrator rights?

To install for all users under Program Files, to apply an optimization, or to restart the network adapter. Monitoring and diagnostics run without them, and every Administrator action asks through the Windows UAC prompt.

Is it safe to change these settings?

Every switch saves the original value first, verifies the result and rolls back on failure. You can restore everything in one go, and riskier tweaks are labelled experimental.

Does it send my data anywhere?

No. To measure your connection it contacts a few public services (your router, Cloudflare, Google DNS) and nothing else. Every connection is listed in the privacy policy.

Why does Windows show a warning when I open it?

Releases are not code-signed: it is a free project and a certificate is a paid service. Windows SmartScreen may therefore show “unknown publisher” on first launch — choose More info, then Run anyway. The release is built from the public source by an open workflow, and you can read or build the code yourself.

Is it really free?

Yes. The app is fully functional with no ads and is released under the GNU General Public License v3.0.

What about Mac, iPhone and Android?

Windows only today. Native apps for Apple platforms and Android are planned, with no date yet.

Which languages does it speak?

The app has nine interface languages, and English is the default. Some translations are machine-generated and still need review by native speakers. This website is English only for now.
