The problem

A fast speed test doesn’t mean a stable connection

A speed test measures a short burst of throughput. Browsing, calls and games depend on something else: whether every packet arrives, on time.

Wi‑Fi

Signal is not flow

Bars measure signal strength. A driver hang, interference or a crowded channel can stall your traffic while all four bars stay lit — and Windows often records nothing.

Your provider

Fast at home, slow abroad

Domestic speed can be excellent while some international routes are congested or shaped, or while your provider’s DNS servers are slow. It is not always your equipment.

Time of day

Evening peaks

Shared networks — apartment Wi‑Fi, mobile towers — get busy when everyone is online, and latency and packet loss climb with them.

Ambysto Steady can’t make your provider faster. It tells you which part of the path is failing, and fixes the parts that are yours to fix.

What it does

Measure. Diagnose. Recover. Optimize.

Runs quietly in the system tray. Everything is stored on your PC.

Measure

Continuous monitoring

Pings your router and the Internet every second, plus TCP and HTTP probes every 10 s — so a real outage is told apart from a provider that merely rate-limits ping. Keeps per-minute history for 24 hours, a week and more.

Diagnose

Finds the likely cause

14 checks: driver, signal, channel interference, drop history, DNS, bufferbloat, Wi‑Fi 7/MLO compatibility, a modem still broadcasting Wi‑Fi in bridge mode, antennas blocked by the case or desk, and more. Plain-language steps, with before/after results once you try them.

Recover

Watchdog for silent stalls

Notices when Wi‑Fi reports “connected” but nothing flows, reconnects, then restarts the adapter if needed. Off by default, rate-limited, and it switches itself off if it isn’t helping.

Optimize

Reversible tweaks

Eleven on/off switches — adapter power saving, PCIe power management, TCP port reuse, IPv6 and more. The original value is saved first, checked afterwards, and rolled back on failure. The effect of each change is measured.

Fail over

Backup connection

When your main path loses the Internet and another one works — wired Ethernet, a USB 4G dongle, your phone’s hotspot — it can switch automatically and switch back when the main path is stable. Off by default.

Know

Notifications & log

A notification when an outage lasts 30 seconds or more and when it ends. A full log of incidents, changes you made and everything the watchdog did.

Real data

From 35% packet loss to 0.4%

One home network, one PC. The Wi‑Fi showed four bars the whole time.

Before

107 minutes, one afternoon

34.9% packet loss to the router

  • 64 connection losses
  • ~42 min offline out of 107
  • Windows logged 1 disconnect

After

9.6 hours, overnight

0.39% packet loss to the router

  • 1 connection loss
  • 3 seconds long
  • No driver crashes

What fixed it was not software: switching off the old modem’s own Wi‑Fi, disabling a Wi‑Fi 7 feature (MLO) the PC’s card couldn’t handle, and turning the PC so the card’s antennas weren’t hidden behind a metal desk frame. They were found by measuring — the kind of checks Ambysto Steady runs and the steps it suggests. This is a single case; your results will differ.

Safety & privacy

Built to never make things worse

Changing network settings should be reversible, so every change goes through the same steps: back up the original value, apply, verify, and roll back automatically if it didn’t work. Uninstalling restores everything.

There is no account, no telemetry, no analytics and no advertising. The privacy policy lists every connection the app makes.

Back up

Original values saved before any change, and restored when you switch a tweak off.

Capped

The watchdog acts at most six times an hour and shuts itself off if three attempts in a row don’t help.

Local

The app talks to its own server on 127.0.0.1 with a per-session token. Nothing is reachable from other computers.

Asks first

Anything needing Administrator rights runs separately and asks through the Windows UAC prompt every time.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Will it make my Internet faster?

No. It can’t increase what your provider delivers. It finds what stops you from getting that speed consistently — interference, a misbehaving driver, a settings conflict, slow DNS — and tells you when the problem is on your provider’s side.

Why does it ask for Administrator rights?

Only to apply an optimization or restart the network adapter. Monitoring and diagnostics run without them, and every Administrator action asks through the Windows UAC prompt each time.

Is it safe to change these settings?

Every switch saves the original value first, verifies the result and rolls back on failure. You can restore everything in one go, and riskier tweaks are labelled experimental.

Does it send my data anywhere?

No. To measure your connection it contacts a few public services (your router, Cloudflare, Google DNS) and nothing else. Every connection is listed in the privacy policy.

Why does Windows show a warning when I open it?

Releases are not code-signed: it is a free project and a certificate is a paid service. Windows SmartScreen may therefore show “unknown publisher” on first launch — choose More info, then Run anyway. The release is built from the public source by an open workflow, and you can read or build the code yourself.

Is it really free?

Yes. The app is fully functional with no ads and is released under the GNU General Public License v3.0.

What about Mac, iPhone and Android?

Windows only today. Native apps for Apple platforms and Android are planned, with no date yet.

Which languages does it speak?

The app has nine interface languages, and English is the default. Some translations are machine-generated and still need review by native speakers. This website is English only for now.
